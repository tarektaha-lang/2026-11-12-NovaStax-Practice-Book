## Equations

When this applies, fill spec.equations with one or more {latex, notes} rows (ids are added on save). Else [].

- Essential symbolic relations only (LaTeX formatted in $...$ or $$...$$ when the section uses them).
- Include a row only when it is both (1) load-bearing—if omitted, later writers would likely invent a different governing relation—and (2) a reusable general form students apply, not a filled numeric instance.
- Do not include numeric instances of a general form already captured, intermediate steps, or one-off calculations from a single worked example; apply semantic union across PDFs; else [].
- A row is a relation the textbook explicitly asks students to write, complete, balance, or compute with (e.g., word equations, symbolic chemical equations, or physical governing formulas such as $v = \frac{d}{t}$ or $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$).
- A picture of a balanced chemical equation or structural formula is not a stoichiometry drill and must not become atom-counting or quantitative stoichiometry unless the text explicitly trains that calculation.
- A purely qualitative section keeps `equations` as [].
