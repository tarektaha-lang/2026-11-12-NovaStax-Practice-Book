## Notation: Arabic
The book writes its mathematics in Arabic notation, right to left: Arabic letters for unknowns and variables (س، ص، ع، ن), for points and vertices (أ، ب، ج، د، هـ، و), and Arabic abbreviations for the functions and units it uses (جا، جتا، ظا؛ سم، م، كجم، ل), exactly as the book prints them.
- Write every equation, expression and symbol in LaTeX inside $...$ or $$...$$, with the Arabic letters the book uses in place of Latin ones: `$س^2 - 4 = 0$`, `$\overline{أب}$`, `$\triangle أبج$`. The LaTeX commands themselves keep their ASCII names — \frac, \sqrt, \times, \div, \angle, \overline, ^, _ — only the letters and words in them are Arabic.
- **Write mathematics in logical order, first operand first**, as `$\frac{17}{28} - \frac{11}{28}$` for "seventeen twenty-eighths minus eleven twenty-eighths", however it is laid out on the page. Never reverse an expression to make it look right-to-left in the file: the typesetter mirrors the layout, and an expression reversed by hand is mirrored back to front.
- Arabic words that are part of the mathematics — a unit, a function name, a label — go in \text{...} inside the $...$: `$5\text{ سم}$`, `$\text{جا}\, س$`.
- Where the book writes a symbol in a house style of its own (how it names an angle, a length, a set, a ratio), record that in notations exactly as the book prints it and use it the same way everywhere. Do not substitute the convention of another Arabic curriculum.
- Digits follow the numerals rule above, whatever letters stand beside them.
- A geometric ray, including a secant ray or a tangent ray, is `\overrightarrow{AB}` — never `\vec`. A true vector or unit vector is `\vec{a}` or `\vec{AB}` — never `\overrightarrow`, and never a raw `\overrightharpoon` in the JSON. A line is `\overleftrightarrow{AB}`; a segment is `\overline{AB}`.
