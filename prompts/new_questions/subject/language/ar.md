## Language: Arabic

The book is in Arabic, and so is every exercise, stem, choice, answer and figure description.
Write Modern Standard Arabic in the register the textbook's own questions use, with the
section's own terms and never a synonym from another curriculum.

- A sentence ends with the full stop «.»; a question ends with the Arabic question mark «؟».
  Use the Arabic comma «،» and semicolon «؛». The colon is «:» and is never the last character
  of an exercise. When the last words are a quotation, the stop stays inside the quotes
  (`.»` or `؟»`), never a bare `»`.
- The four choices of a multiple-choice exercise are printed as «(أ)», «(ب)», «(ج)» and «(د)»,
  in the order you give them, and `answer` names the correct one by that letter: `أ`, `ب`, `ج`
  or `د`.
- **Tashkeel precedence.** Apply these in order:
  1. If the exercise is a **tashkeel task**, the exceptions under "Tashkeel tasks" below apply.
  2. Otherwise, **tashkeel is CONTEXTUAL** on printed wording: write each set `stem`, each
     exercise `text`, and every entry in `options` without harakāt by default. Add tashkeel
     only on individual words that have two or more plausible readings in that context
     (تشكيل الملبس). Do not add case-ending marks unless the task is about them. Do not
     vocalise whole sentences. Do not sprinkle marks across a sentence because they are
     optional. Keep the same light style across the stem, wording and choices of one set —
     never a fully vocalised item beside bare neighbours. Inside `﴿…﴾` and inside `as_is`
     excerpts, keep the source marks. Poetry and other non-sacred quotes go in `«…»` and stay
     bare unless CONTEXTUAL disambiguation or `as_is` requires marks.
  - Good: `تولَّى محمد علي حكم مصر.` (one clarifying shadda+vowel; the rest bare).
  - Bad: `تَوَلَّى مُحَمَّدٌ عَلِيٌّ حُكْمَ مِصْرَ.` (full-sentence vocalisation).
  - When you add a mark, place it correctly:
    - Never leave a **bare shadda** (شدة معراة): if you write shadda, pair it with the needed
      haraka or tanween on that letter (`بيِّن`, not `بيّن`).
    - **Fatḥatān** sits on the letter before alif (`دائمًا`), never on the alif itself
      (`دائماً`).
    - An **adverbial accusative** that ends in alif needs fatḥatān (`نطقًا`, not bare
      `نطقا` and not `نطقاً`).
    - A **nisba** adjective with tanween needs shadda on yā' (`شرعيًّا`, `إلهيًّا`), not
      `شرعيًا`. Do not add that shadda on non-nisba forms such as `خاليًا` / `كافيًا`.
    - Mark the **letter that resolves** the reading (`بالنَّسب`, not `بالنسَب`).
    - When the meaning is “notice / feel”, use Form I `يَشعر` (not `يُشعر`) unless the
      task itself is about that verb form.
    - The imperative “clarify / show” is always `بيِّن` (shadda + kasra). Do not mix
      `بيِّن` and bare `بين` as that verb in one section. The preposition `بين` (between)
      stays bare.
- **Quoted wording uses two wrappers — do not mix them and do not nest them.**
  - **Ayah / sacred citation** (`قوله تعالى`, a recited verse or fixed sacred quote): always
    `﴿…﴾` with the harakāt the section excerpt prints. Never put a sacred citation in `«…»`.
  - **Word or phrase under discussion** (after `كلمة`, `لفظة`, `تركيب`, or a lone item to
    classify / define / find the root): always `«…»`, even when the string comes from the
    Qur'an. Do not upgrade a lemma label to `﴿…﴾`. Never use parentheses `(…)` for that
    word. Prefer company wording such as `الكلمة المحددة` (not `بين قوسين`, and not the
    awkward `بين «…»` as a stem phrase).
  - **Lemma beside an ayah** (do not nest): name the word in `«…»`, then cite the verse in
    `﴿…﴾`. Never put `«…»` inside `﴿…﴾` or the reverse.
  - **Other non-sacred quotes** (textbook prose, dialogue, poetry): `«…»`, bare unless
    CONTEXTUAL disambiguation or `as_is` requires marks. Never upgrade them to `﴿…﴾`.
  - Instruction and non-quote choices follow the CONTEXTUAL rule above.
  - Good: `ما معنى كلمة «المراضع» في قوله تعالى: ﴿وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَرَاضِعَ مِنْ قَبْلُ﴾؟`
  - Good: `صنف كلمة «أرضعيه» في ﴿وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَى أُمِّ مُوسَى أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ﴾.`
  - Good (جذر / معنى / اسم·فعل·حرف with the item alone as ayah material): `﴿أَوْحَيْنَا﴾` as
    the whole exercise text when the set stem already says the task — that *is* a citation.
  - Bad: `ما معنى كلمة ﴿الْمَرَاضِعَ﴾…` (lemma labeled as mushaf) /
    `قوله تعالى: «وحرمنا عليه المراضع»` (ayah in guillemets, marks stripped) /
    `بيِّن قسم الكلمة التي بين (المراضع)` (parentheses instead of `«…»`) /
    `﴿… «المراضع» …﴾` (wrappers nested)
- **Ayah sourcing.** Cite only ayahs that appear in the section excerpts, and copy their marks
  from there. Never write or vocalise an ayah from memory. If no matching ayah is available,
  do not invent one and do not drop the exercise: write it without a verse citation, or
  re-target it to a word or phrase that does appear in the excerpts, and name that word in
  `«…»`. If the exercise is inherently about one specific ayah, re-target it the same way.
- **Tashkeel tasks.** An exercise is a tashkeel task if its answer is a vocalisation: تشكيل,
  ضبط بالحركات, choosing the correct haraka, or any similar task. Only for these exercises:
  - **Stem.** Never print the winning harakāt on the tested word. Name the target bare in
    `«…»` (`«المراضع»`, `«الكتاب»`, `«إلا»`). If an ayah is cited, the rest of the ayah keeps
    its source marks; on the tested word only, omit the contested mark, replace the word with
    a blank, or omit the word. This overrides "copy the ayah's marks" for the tested word only.
  - **Options.** Each option is vocalised on the tested word only; everything else in the
    option follows the contextual rule.
  - **`answer`, multiple choice.** The vocalised forms live only in `options`. `answer` is the
    letter alone (`أ` / `ب` / `ج` / `د`), never a letter followed by a vocalised word.
  - **`answer`, free response.** The key carries the vocalised form the student was asked to
    supply or identify.
  - These rules hold with or without an ayah. Not having an ayah never relaxes the no-leak rule.
  - Good: `ما الضبط الصحيح لكلمة «المراضع» في قوله تعالى: ﴿وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ …﴾؟`
  - Bad: `ما الضبط الصحيح لكلمة ﴿الْمَرَاضِعَ﴾ في ﴿وَحَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَرَاضِعَ﴾؟`
  - In every other exercise, `answer` follows the same CONTEXTUAL rule as the stem (no full
    vocalisation; marks only where needed to read the key).
- Write every field in logical order, exactly as it is read. Never insert direction marks or
  bidi control characters (U+200E, U+200F, U+202A–U+202E, U+2066–U+2069): the paper is set
  right to left by the typesetter, and a control character in a field is one it cannot render.
- Arabic words that stand inside mathematics — a unit, a function name — go in `\text{...}`
  inside the `$...$`: `$5\text{ سم}$`. Mathematics stands inside `$...$` and the Arabic wording
  outside it, and the two are never mixed in one run of characters.
- A blank is `\blank`, exactly as the rules above say, wherever in the sentence it falls:
  `طول الضلع المجهول يساوي $\blank$ سم.`
- Word problems use the names, places, currency and units of the book's own country, written
  in Arabic as its textbook writes them. A name the book prints in Latin letters stays in Latin
  letters.
- Figure descriptions are written in Arabic as well, naming points and values with the
  letters and digits the numerals and notation rules give.
