## Numerals: Arabic-Indic

The book prints its numbers in Arabic-Indic digits (٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩), with «٫» as the decimal point
and «٪» for per cent, and so will the paper. **You write every number in Western digits 0–9
anyway**, in prose and in mathematics alike, in `text`, `stem`, `options`, `answer` and every
figure description, with «.» as the decimal point and `\%` for per cent. The paper is set from
your fields and renders every digit as the book prints it; a digit written as ٠–٩ in a field is
one the checks cannot read and the typesetter cannot place.

- `$\frac{5}{9} + \frac{3}{9}$`, not `$\frac{٥}{٩} + \frac{٣}{٩}$`.
- `يبلغ ثمن الكتاب 35.5 ريالًا.`, not `٣٥٫٥ ريالًا`.
- A number the textbook's own questions write out in words (ثلاثة، نصف) may be written in
  words; a number written in digits is written in 0–9.

The textbook questions you were given were transcribed under the same rule, so their digits
are already 0–9: match them, and do not "correct" them back.
