## Language: Arabic

The book is in Arabic, and so is every field of this exercise: stem, wording, choices, answer and figure descriptions. Work the Arabic as printed. Your reasons stay in English, as the output rule says.

- Judge the Modern Standard Arabic as printed — its letter forms, its tashkeel, its punctuation («،» «؛» «؟» «.»). A wording issue is a sentence the student cannot answer from, not a phrasing you would have preferred, and not a hamza or a yeh you would have written differently.
- **Tashkeel on practice wording is CONTEXTUAL (تشكيل الملبس).** Sparse marks on words with two or more plausible readings in context are correct — do not flag them as defects. Case-ending marks on ordinary practice prose are a wording defect unless the task is about them. Heavy or full-sentence tashkeel on stem, text, or choices outside a Quranic `﴿…﴾` quote or an `as_is` excerpt is a wording defect, unless the exercise is a tashkeel task (تشكيل / ضبط / choosing the correct haraka) and the marks are only the competing forms on the tested word in `options`. Do not keep full vocalisation because it is grammatical.
- Mark-placement defects on practice wording: a bare shadda with no paired haraka/tanween (`بيّن` instead of `بيِّن`); fatḥatān on alif instead of on the letter before it; a missing fatḥatān on an adverbial accusative (`نطقا` instead of `نطقًا`); a nisba with tanween but no shadda on yā' (`شرعيًا` instead of `شرعيًّا` — do not flag `خاليًا` / `كافيًا`); mixing imperative `بيِّن` with bare `بين` as the same “clarify” verb in one section (the preposition `بين` may stay bare); a disambiguation mark on the wrong letter (e.g. `بالنسَب` where `بالنَّسب` is required).
- Quote wrappers: an ayah / `قوله تعالى` citation must be `﴿…﴾` with source harakāt copied
  from the section excerpts — bare `«…»`, stripped marks, or an ayah invented from memory
  is a wording defect. A lemma under discussion (`كلمة`, `لفظة`, classify / معنى / جذر)
  must be `«…»`, not `﴿…﴾` and not parentheses `(…)`, even when the string is Quranic.
  Prefer `الكلمة المحددة` over `بين قوسين`. Do not nest `«…»` inside `﴿…﴾` or the reverse;
  name the lemma beside the ayah.
- If the task is تشكيل / ضبط, the stem must not print the winning harakāt on the tested word
  (Quranic or not), with or without an ayah. A fully vocalised target in the stem, or an
  ayah that already shows the contested mark on that word, is a wording defect. For MCQs,
  vocalised forms live only in `options`; `answer` must be the letter alone.
- The four choices of a multiple-choice exercise are «(أ)», «(ب)», «(ج)» and «(د)», and `answer` names the correct one by that letter: `أ`, `ب`, `ج` or `د`. `(أ)`, `أ` and `أ، لأن …` are the same key; do not report how the letter is written.
- `$...$` is only for mathematics. An Arabic word that is not a variable, a point name,
  or part of a formula stays outside `$...$`: write `افهم`, never `$افهم$`. `$افهم$` is a
  wording defect.
- An Arabic word that *does* stand inside a formula — a unit, a function name, a
  multi-letter abbreviation such as مح — goes in `\text{...}` inside the `$...$`:
  `$5\text{ سم}$`, `$\text{مح} = 48\text{ سم}$`. Bare `$مح$` is a wording defect: the
  paper prints isolated glyphs, not the word.
- The fields are written in logical order. Direction marks and bidi control characters are a typesetting fault, not a mathematical one; do not report them.
